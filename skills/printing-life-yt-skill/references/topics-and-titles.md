# Printing Life — Topics and Titles

The channel's lane is **history with a human face and a ridiculous detail**:
wars, revolutions, empires, scams, accidents and the people who caused them.
Big enough to matter, small enough to explain, strange enough to click.

Do not become a generic "history facts" channel. Every episode needs a **story
with a turn**, not a list of events.

---

## 1. The topic scorecard

Score each candidate 1–5. Ship at **26+/35**.

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| Recognition | Does a 15-year-old recognise the name or the object? |
| Stakes | Did it change borders, money, religion or how people live? |
| Characters | Are there 3–6 people with clear wants and flaws? |
| Visuality | Can it be told with halls, maps, crowds, uniforms and one prop? |
| Twist | Is there a reversal the audience does not expect? |
| Freshness | Is YouTube already saturated with the same angle? |
| Safety | Can it be told without glorifying atrocity or punching down? |

If **Recognition** is 1–2, the title must carry the curiosity alone ("The War of
the Stray Dog"). If **Safety** is 1–2, drop it. Some stories are important but
wrong for this format.

## 2. The four lanes

### Lane A — Small Wars (the growth engine)

A stupid dispute with a real consequence. Cheap to make, highly clickable,
perfect for a new channel.

**Rules:** one sitting, 6–9 minutes, 2–4 characters, one map, one absurd object.
The war must be real and the consequence must be real. The comedy is in the
proportionality.

**Ideas**

| Topic | The ridiculous detail | Real consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **The Pastry War** (1838) | A French pastry chef claims Mexican officers ate his cakes; France sends a fleet | Mexico is forced to pay; Santa Anna loses a leg |
| **The Toledo War** (1835) | Ohio and Michigan fight over a strip of swamp; one man is stabbed | Michigan becomes a state; the Upper Peninsula is the consolation prize |
| **The War of the Stray Dog** (1925) | A Greek soldier chases his dog across the Bulgarian border and is shot | Greece invades Bulgaria; the League of Nations has to intervene |
| **The Lobster War** (1961) | Brazil and France argue whether lobsters "swim" or "crawl" | Warships deploy over a crustacean; the dispute rumbles for decades |
| **The Honey War** (1839) | Missouri and Iowa tax each other's honey trees | A border is drawn; nobody dies; the "war" is three sheriff arrests |
| **The Cod Wars** (1958–76) | Iceland and Britain fight over fish with cutters and trawlers | Iceland wins; the Royal Navy is humiliated; the sea changes |
| **The War of the Golden Stool** (1900) | A British governor demands to sit on a sacred stool | A colonial war, a queen mother, and a symbol that outlives the empire |
| **The Pig War** | *Already a famous OverSimplified episode. Skip; do not retrace.* | — |

### Lane B — Profiles

One person, one obsession, one consequence. The audience clicks for the face;
they stay for the psychology.

**Rules:** 8–12 minutes. Open on the person at their most extreme, then rewind.
Do not worship and do not diagnose from the armchair. Give them a clear want and
a clear cost.

**Ideas**

| Topic | Hook |
|---|---|
| **King Zog of Albania** | The only modern king who survived 55 assassination attempts, ran a casino, and smoked through a revolution |
| **Empress Matilda** | She was denied a throne, fought a 19-year civil war, and won by outliving everyone |
| **The False Dmitris** | Three different men claimed to be the same dead prince; Russia believed at least one |
| **Mansa Musa** | The richest man in history accidentally crashed the gold market on his way to Mecca |
| **Zheng He** | China had the largest fleet on earth, then burned it and banned the ocean |
| **Emperor Norton** | A bankrupt businessman declared himself Emperor of the United States; the city played along |
| **Toussaint Louverture** | A formerly enslaved man built the only successful slave revolution in history |
| **Queen Nzinga** | She negotiated with Portugal, fought them for decades, and refused to sit lower than a king |
| **Napoleon III** | The other Napoleon, who tried to be his uncle and accidentally created Germany |
| **Rasputin** | *OverSimplified covers the Russian Revolution. If you do him, angle on the court, not the war.* |

### Lane C — Main stories (the tentpoles)

Famous wars and revolutions, split into two parts. These are the videos that
define the channel, but they are expensive. Do not start here.

**Rules:** 10–16 minutes per part. The split is a decision point, not a halfway
marker. Part 1 promises; Part 2 pays.

**Ideas**

| Topic | Why it works | Two-part split |
|---|---|---|
| **The Haitian Revolution** | Enslaved people defeat three empires; enormous stakes, undersold on YouTube | The uprising / The price of independence |
| **The Thirty Years' War** | A religious argument turns Europe into a slaughterhouse; wildly visual | The defenestration / The exhaustion |
| **The Crusades** | Famous, strange, full of characters and logistics | The first wave / The long failure |
| **The Wars of the Roses** | A family feud with crowns, betrayals and convenient disappearances | The claim / The Tudors |
| **The Meiji Restoration** | A country reinvents itself in 30 years; uniforms, trains, samurai | The Black Ships / The last samurai |
| **The Fall of Constantinople** | The end of an empire, a wall, a cannon, one very bad decision | The siege / The day after |
| **The Opium Wars** | Trade, addiction, gunboats and humiliation; must be handled soberly | The trade / The treaties |
| **The Scramble for Africa** | Huge, important, visually map-driven; requires care and no adventure-story framing | The conference / The resistance |
| **The Great Game** | Spies, maps, mountains and two empires playing chess badly | The approach / The pawns |

### Lane D — Origin stories (the channel's signature series)

How an ordinary object or system quietly redistributed power. This is where the
name **Printing Life** earns its keep.

**Rules:** 8–12 minutes. The object is the protagonist. Every act answers: who
controlled it, who it empowered, who it hurt, and what it replaced.

**Ideas**

| Topic | The turn |
|---|---|
| **The printing press** | A goldsmith tries to make cheap mirrors and accidentally makes the Reformation possible |
| **The passport** | A wartime temporary measure becomes the permanent gatekeeper of the world |
| **Standard time** | Trains kill people because every town has its own noon; time becomes a bureaucracy |
| **The telegraph** | The first instant message destroys the messenger, the diplomat and the price of wheat |
| **The banknote** | Paper becomes money because a king promises, and everyone slowly believes him |
| **The census** | Counting people is how states learn to tax, conscript and control them |
| **The map** | The blank spaces on a map are an invitation; the lines are a claim |
| **The newspaper** | The first mass medium invents the public, the scandal and the headline |
| **The barcode** | A supermarket line becomes a global standard for everything |
| **The typewriter** | It opens offices to women and locks the keyboard into QWERTY |

Start with **the printing press**. It is the brand statement, it is visually
rich, and it sets up every later episode about information and power.

## 3. Titles

### Main stories

```
The French Revolution - Printing Life (Part 1)
The French Revolution - Printing Life (Part 2)
The Thirty Years' War - Printing Life (Part 1)
```

Use the **canonical name**, not a clever paraphrase. Search is the point.
`(Part 1)` is a promise, not a teaser.

### Small Wars

```
The Pastry War - Small Wars #1
The Toledo War - Small Wars #2
```

The series number builds a playlist and a binge habit. Do **not** reuse
OverSimplified's "MiniWars" name or numbering; make the series ours.

### Profiles and Origins

```
King Zog - Printing Life
The Printing Press - Printing Life
```

Optional subtitle for clarity, not clickbait:

```
The Printing Press - Printing Life | How a Gold Mirror Broke the Church
```

Keep the part before the pipe short. YouTube truncates around 60 characters on
mobile; the canonical name must survive first.

### Title rules

- **Front-load the noun.** "The Pastry War", not "The War That Started Over a
  Pastry".
- **No question titles.** Questions test worse for this format and attract
  low-intent clicks.
- **No all-caps.** The thumbnail is loud; the title can be calm.
- **No fake stakes.** "The War That Almost Ended the World" is banned unless it
  actually almost ended the world.
- **One number maximum.** "The 335-Year War" is fine. "10 Shocking Facts" is not
  this channel.
- **Spell the name right.** Search traffic dies on a typo.

## 4. Thumbnail concept by lane

| Lane | Background | Characters | Title | Extra |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Small Wars | The absurd object, large | Two angry officials or one confused soldier | 2–3 words, the object or place | A tiny map arrow |
| Profile | The person's silhouette-defining room | One close-up, extreme expression | The name | A crown, a knife, a ledger |
| Main story | A battle or a palace | Two rivals facing off | The event, 2–4 words | `PART 1` badge |
| Origin | The object, dramatic light | One person holding or hiding it | The object | A "before/after" split |

The thumbnail and title must not repeat the same words. Title: "The Pastry War".
Thumbnail: "CAKE → WAR". Title: "The Printing Press". Thumbnail: "ONE MACHINE".

## 5. The first ten

A realistic slate for a new channel. One video every **2–3 weeks**, not weekly.
Quality is the strategy; a rushed episode teaches the algorithm the wrong lesson.

| # | Episode | Lane | Why now |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Pastry War** | Small Wars #1 | Cheap, funny, complete story, perfect format proof |
| 2 | **The Printing Press** | Origin | Brand anchor; ties the channel name to the content |
| 3 | **The Toledo War** | Small Wars #2 | Zero deaths, high absurdity, strong thumbnail |
| 4 | **King Zog** | Profile | A face and a personality; tests character-led retention |
| 5 | **The Haitian Revolution (Part 1)** | Main | First tentpole; serious, important, visually strong |
| 6 | **The Haitian Revolution (Part 2)** | Main | Payoff; tests the two-part binge |
| 7 | **The Lobster War** | Small Wars #3 | Light palate cleanser after the epic |
| 8 | **The Thirty Years' War (Part 1)** | Main | Biggest swing; only after the pipeline is proven |
| 9 | **The Thirty Years' War (Part 2)** | Main | Completion of the tentpole |
| 10 | **The Great Game** | Main | Maps, spies, mountains; a new visual palette |

Do not start with the Thirty Years' War. It is the reward for finishing the first
seven, not the test of whether the channel works.

## 6. What not to cover yet

- **WW1, WW2, the Cold War, the Russian Revolution, the French Revolution,
  Napoleon, the American Civil War, the Punic Wars.** These are the core of
  OverSimplified's catalogue. Competing head-on with a 9.6M-sub channel on its
  strongest topics is a bad first move. You can cover them later with a distinct
  angle, but not in the first ten.
- **Recent atrocities with living victims.** No jokes, no thumbnails, no
  "forgotten genocide" framing.
- **Conspiracy bait.** No "the truth they hid", no secret societies as fact.
- **Modern partisan politics.** The channel is history, not a campaign.
- **A topic you cannot source in two days.** If the research file is thin, the
  script will invent; that is how history channels die.

## 8. Research prompts

When a topic is chosen, write `research.md` with:

```markdown
# Topic
## The one-sentence story
## Why it matters (the consequence)
## Cast (3–6 people, one want and one flaw each)
## Timeline (only the dates that will appear on screen)
## Five set pieces (visual scenes, not abstract events)
## The twist (what the audience will not expect)
## The bill (what changed, who paid)
## Sources (two per major claim)
## Sensitive material and how it is handled
```
